# Study Notes Formatting Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines detailed rules for transforming raw study material into clear, accurate, professional, and exam-focused notes.

The main skill should consult this guide when detailed formatting or subject-specific guidance is needed.

---

## 1. Student Level

Always adapt the language and depth to the student's level.

### Class 6–8

* Use simple and familiar vocabulary.
* Explain difficult scientific terms in easy language.
* Keep definitions short and easy to memorize.
* Use simple examples from everyday life.
* Avoid unnecessary technical detail.
* Break complex concepts into small points.
* Focus on understanding before memorization.

### Class 9–10

* Use proper textbook and scientific terminology.
* Give precise, exam-appropriate definitions.
* Include important formulas, units, laws, principles, and relationships.
* Explain concepts clearly but do not over-simplify them.
* Include short examples where useful.
* Highlight points that are likely to be important for examinations.
* Maintain enough detail for written exam answers.

---

## 2. Definitions

Definitions should be:

* Scientifically accurate.
* Clear and concise.
* Suitable for the student's level.
* Written as complete sentences.
* Easy to revise and memorize.

Prefer:

> **Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance.**

Avoid incomplete definitions such as:

> Density — mass present in a certain volume.

When a technical term is necessary, use the correct scientific terminology and explain it separately if needed.

Do not make definitions unnecessarily long.

---

## 3. Physics Formulas

For Physics notes:

* Write the formula clearly.
* Use standard symbols.
* Define every symbol used in the formula.
* Mention the SI unit of the quantity.
* Include a short numerical example when appropriate.
* Keep mathematical notation clear.

Example:

### Density

$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V}
$$

Where:

* \(\rho\) = density
* \(m\) = mass
* \(V\) = volume

SI unit:

$$
kg/m^3
$$

If a numerical example is included, show the calculation step by step.

Example:

Mass = 200 g
Volume = 50 cm³

$$
Density = \frac{Mass}{Volume}
$$

$$
Density = \frac{200}{50} = 4\ g/cm^3
$$

Do not introduce formulas that are unrelated to the supplied topic.

---

## 4. Chemistry Equations

For Chemistry:

* Use correct chemical symbols and formulas.
* Use proper subscripts where required.
* Balance chemical equations when balancing is part of the topic.
* Identify reactants and products clearly.
* Do not change the chemical meaning of the source material.
* Explain unfamiliar symbols or terms when necessary.

Example:

Unbalanced:

$$
H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow H_2O
$$

Balanced:

$$
2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O
$$

When relevant, mention whether the equation represents a reaction such as combination, decomposition, displacement, or combustion.

Do not invent chemical equations when the source does not provide enough information to establish one safely.

---

## 5. Biology Processes

For Biology processes:

* Present processes in logical sequence.
* Use numbered steps when order matters.
* Explain important terms in simple language.
* Mention the purpose or result of the process.
* Include important conditions or factors when provided in the source.
* Use arrows when they make the sequence easier to understand.

Example:

### Photosynthesis

1. Roots absorb water from the soil.
2. Water is transported to the leaves.
3. Carbon dioxide enters the leaf through stomata.
4. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy.
5. Light energy is used to produce glucose.
6. Oxygen is released into the atmosphere.

For complex processes, separate:

* **What happens**
* **Where it happens**
* **What is required**
* **What is produced**

Do not add unsupported biological details.

---

## 6. Important Points

Create an **Important Points** section when it improves revision.

Select points that are:

* Essential to understanding the topic.
* Important for examinations.
* Definitions or key facts.
* Formulas or units.
* Important differences or relationships.
* Steps of an important process.
* Conditions or factors that affect a process.
* Facts that students commonly need to remember.

Do not repeat the entire notes in the Important Points section.

Prefer 4–8 concise points when the topic allows.

Example:

### Important Points

* Density is mass per unit volume.
* The SI unit of density is kg/m³.
* Density is calculated using \(\rho = m/V\).
* Water has a density of approximately 1 g/cm³.
* Density can help identify substances.

---

## 7. Review Questions

Add review questions at the end when appropriate.

Questions should test understanding rather than simply repeat sentences from the notes.

Use a mixture of question types when suitable:

* Definition questions.
* Short-answer questions.
* Conceptual questions.
* Formula-based questions.
* Application questions.
* Comparison questions.
* Simple numerical questions.

### One Main Concept Per Question

Each question should normally test one main idea.

Avoid questions that combine several unrelated requirements.

Weak:

> Define density, give its SI unit, write its formula, explain water's density, and describe how density identifies materials.

Better:

1. What is density?
2. Write the formula for density.
3. What is the SI unit of density?
4. What is the approximate density of water?
5. How can density help identify a material?

Questions should match the student's level.

Do not make questions unnecessarily difficult.

---

## 8. Handling Factual Mistakes in Raw Notes

Raw notes may contain spelling mistakes, grammar problems, incomplete statements, or factual errors.

The skill should:

1. Identify obvious errors.
2. Correct grammar and spelling.
3. Correct scientifically established factual errors.
4. Preserve the intended meaning when possible.
5. Never knowingly reproduce an incorrect scientific statement.
6. Avoid inventing information to fill gaps.
7. If a statement is ambiguous or cannot be safely corrected, preserve the idea cautiously and indicate that clarification is needed.

### Example

Raw note:

> Density means volume divided by mass.

Corrected:

> **Density is mass per unit volume.**

Formula:

$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V}
$$

The skill should prioritize accuracy over blindly preserving incorrect wording.

---

## 9. Avoiding Repetition

Do not repeat the same information unnecessarily.

For example, if the definition already explains that density is mass per unit volume, do not repeat the exact same sentence several times.

Different sections should have different purposes:

* **Definition** → What the concept means.
* **Explanation** → How or why it works.
* **Example** → Demonstrates the concept.
* **Important Points** → Quick revision.
* **Review Questions** → Tests understanding.

---

## 10. Notes Should Be Exam-Friendly

Professional notes should be:

* Well organized.
* Easy to scan.
* Easy to revise.
* Scientifically accurate.
* Appropriate for the student's class level.
* Concise but sufficiently explained.

Use:

* Headings.
* Subheadings.
* Bullet points.
* Numbered steps.
* Bold key terms.
* Proper formulas.
* Tables only when they genuinely improve comparison.

Avoid:

* Large unnecessary paragraphs.
* Excessive decoration.
* Repeated information.
* Unnecessary emojis.
* Overly advanced vocabulary.
* Unsupported facts.

---

## 11. Subject-Specific Priority

When subject-specific formatting rules conflict with general formatting, prioritize scientific accuracy and the conventions of that subject.

### Physics

Prioritize:

* Definitions.
* Laws and principles.
* Formulas.
* Symbols.
* SI units.
* Relationships between quantities.
* Numerical examples.

### Chemistry

Prioritize:

* Definitions.
* Chemical symbols and formulas.
* Equations.
* Valency and ions when relevant.
* Laws and principles.
* Reactions.
* Conditions of reactions when provided.

### Biology

Prioritize:

* Definitions.
* Structures.
* Functions.
* Processes.
* Sequences and stages.
* Diagrams or labels when supplied.
* Important biological terminology.

---

## 12. Final Quality Check

Before producing the final notes, check:

* Is the information scientifically accurate?
* Is the language appropriate for the student's class?
* Are definitions precise?
* Are formulas correct?
* Are units included where relevant?
* Are chemical equations correctly written?
* Are biological processes in the correct order?
* Has unnecessary repetition been removed?
* Are important points easy to revise?
* Do review questions test the actual topic?
* Has unsupported information been avoided?
* Is the final output clean and professional?
